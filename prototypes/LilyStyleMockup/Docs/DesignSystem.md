# Design system: tokens and reusable components

These are the pieces the prototype screens are built from. They live in `LilyStyleMockup/DesignSystem/` and are meant to carry forward into the real app. The look follows PRD §7.1: a warm editorial styling notebook, garment-led, with ordinary iOS controls.

## Color tokens

Colors are asset-catalog color sets with light and dark appearances (`Resources/Assets.xcassets`), exposed in code as `Palette.*`. Garment colors are separate: they come from each garment's confirmed hex value and look the same in both appearances.

| Token (`Palette.`) | Light | Dark | Use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `background` | `#FAF7F2` | `#1C191B` | Screen background |
| `surface` | `#FFFFFF` | `#292529` | Cards, sheets, inputs |
| `primaryText` | `#29242A` | `#FAF7F2` | Headings, body, key labels |
| `secondaryText` | `#6E626A` | `#C5B8C0` | Explanations, metadata |
| `primaryAction` | `#633B55` | `#D9AFCB` | Style Me, selected controls, key actions |
| `onPrimaryAction` | `#FFFFFF` | `#1C191B` | Text/icons on plum fills |
| `accentSurface` | `#F0E0E5` | `#432F3D` | Selected chips, quiet emphasis |
| `success` | `#42634E` | `#AFCEB6` | Ownership confirmation, saves |
| `successSurface` | `#E7EFE8` | `#273B2D` | Already Own badges |
| `divider` | `#DED5DA` | `#51464F` | Decorative separators only |
| `error` | `#A12D39` | `#FFB3B8` | Errors, destructive actions |
| `imageWell` *(derived)* | `#F3EEE8` | `#353035` | Neutral surface behind garment art |
| `controlBorder` *(derived)* | `#8C7F88` | `#8F838B` | Outlines of chips, buttons and inputs |
| `figure` *(derived)* | `#BDB4B9` | `#5A5259` | The placeholder mannequin in On Me previews (decorative) |

The last three are additions, not part of the PRD palette. `imageWell` gives garments a consistent neutral backdrop, and `figure` keeps the placeholder mannequin on a token instead of a raw hex value. `controlBorder` exists because the PRD's divider color is only about 1.4:1 against surfaces, and §7.1 says the divider can't be the only boundary of a control. Every pairing used for text passes 4.5:1 in both appearances, and the control outline passes 3:1. The full table is in [contrast-table.md](contrast-table.md), generated by `Tools/contrast_check.py` from the asset catalog.

## Type, spacing and shape

- System font with Dynamic Type text styles everywhere. `Font.editorial(_:)` gives the serif design and is reserved for the app title, lane names and a few editorial headings.
- `Spacing`: 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48. Screen margins and card padding are 16.
- `Radius.card` 16, `Radius.tile` 12, `Radius.small` 8. Occasion and filter chips are capsules.
- `HitTarget.minimum` is 44 pt, and `.minimumHitTarget()` enforces it on small icon buttons.
- `WidthClass(width:)` classifies the space a view actually gets: compact under 600 pt, intermediate up to 979, wide from 980. Screens decide layout from this, not from the device name or orientation.

## Motion

`DesignSystem/Motion.swift` keeps animation in one vocabulary so screens stay calm:

| API | Use | With Reduce Motion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Motion.standard`, `.layout`, `.quick` | Selection and badge changes; cards and panes; press feedback | `Motion.animation(_:reduceMotion:)` swaps in a short cross-fade |
| `Motion.cardTransition`, `.swapTransition` | Cards and messages appearing; one garment replacing another | Opacity only |
| `.staggeredAppear(index:)` | Lists that arrive together (suggested questions, result cards) | Plain fade, no offset |
| `.pressFeedback` button style | Tappable cards, tiles and chips | No scale |
| `.symbolBounce(on:)` | One-off icon feedback (send, save) | Skipped |
| `WorkingDots`, `.pendingPulse`, `ProgressRing` | Stylist thinking, pending pictures, image-job progress | Static dots, no pulse, no ring animation |

Haptics follow Apple's guidance to use them only for their usual meaning: a selection tick for chips and swaps, a light impact on Send, success when looks or answers arrive or a save succeeds, and a warning for error toasts. They go through `Haptics` (`DesignSystem/Haptics.swift`), which wraps UIKit's feedback generators and is called from button actions and `onChange`. The prototype doesn't use SwiftUI's `.sensoryFeedback` modifier: on iPad its location-based feedback crashed the app when a chip's selection changed during a layout rebuild (see [Verification.md](Verification.md)). Calling haptics from actions also keeps programmatic changes, such as a restored request or a launch route, silent.

## Rules the components follow

- Meaning never depends on color alone: badges carry text and an icon, lanes carry names, and selection adds a checkmark or outline.
- Animations are short and respect Reduce Motion.
- Garment colors are never filtered or tinted. Representative art and text-only items are labeled as such.
- Simulated actions say so. A billing, export, sync or retailer action that is out of scope shows a review state, not a fake success.
- Tappable controls have a 44 pt hit area even when the visible shape is smaller, and their outline uses `controlBorder` rather than the decorative divider.

## Keyboard

On iPad with a hardware keyboard, the Styling menu offers Style Me (Shift-Command-Return), Search (Command-F) and Add Item (Command-N). They're disabled while a sheet is open. Command-Return on its own belongs to the screen's main action: Style Me on the form, Send in the chat, Review in Laundry, Search in Find One and Submit in Feedback. Closet, Saved and Feedback bind Command-F or Command-N to their own buttons, and the global command steps aside there. Command-F returns to an open Search instead of stacking a second one.
